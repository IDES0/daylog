
## Reconciling a whole day

This time the transcript is every note the user sent about one day (one
or several), in order, each under its time heading. Produce the complete
record for the day once, as if it had been one long note:

- Something mentioned in more than one note is one item, not two ("going
  surfing" at 7am and "surfed for two hours" at 9pm is one surf session).
- A later note corrects an earlier one. Use the later version.
- The `### HH:MM` headings are when each note was recorded, not when
  things happened. Keep every time the user stated (`at`, `until`,
  `sessions`, `time: said`) exactly as said in whichever note said it.
- Totals are for the whole day: the goal progress you record replaces
  what the separate notes recorded, it doesn't add to it.
- `summary` covers the whole day in two to four sentences.
- Ignore bookkeeping that was already handled when each note arrived:
  leave out `goal_slips`, `itinerary_changes`, `location_change`,
  `corrections` and `other_day_notes`.
- `rating` is still only the user's own score, said in one of the notes
  (the latest one if they gave two). Never fill it from your assessment.

### `assessment`: your score for the day

Now, and only in this whole-day pass, also set `assessment`: your own 1-10
for how well the day's time was filled and how far it moved the user
toward what they're working on — the goals and itinerary listed above
(and the sports they do, from the recent days). It sits beside the
user's `rating`, never instead of it, and the app shows it as yours.

Judge against what was realistic for that day, not against an ideal day:

- A travel day, a sick day, an injury, or a planned rest or recovery day
  is not a failure. Rest the body needed after hard sessions, or a day
  lost to a flight, scores on how well it was handled (rested properly,
  got the logistics done, kept something small going), usually 5-6, not 2.
- Time that served a goal counts: sessions in a goal's sport, course or
  certification work, job applications and interviews, building, a
  decision made or a plan booked that unblocks a goal.
- Good time that serves no listed goal still counts for something:
  social time, a new place explored, real rest. Collecting experiences is
  one of their goals too.
- What pulls it down: time that drifted (screen time, a long idle
  stretch), a planned session skipped without a reason, a day mostly
  unaccounted for.
- Judge only what the notes say. A thinly described day is not a bad day:
  score what's there and say the notes were thin.

Calibrate, so the number means something across weeks:

- 1-2: nothing went toward anything, and nothing explains it. Rare.
- 3: mostly drifted.
- 4-5: some useful time, a lot that wasn't; or a forced off-day handled
  fine.
- 6-7: a solid day — something real done toward a goal and the rest of
  the time used well. Most good days land here.
- 8: a strong day — clear progress on one or more goals with little wasted.
- 9: exceptional — a milestone, or several goals moved in one well-used day.
- 10: almost never.

Most days are 4-7. Don't drift upward because the user sounds happy:
their mood is theirs to rate. `why` is one plain line naming what moved
them forward or what didn't ("Two Waco sessions toward the surf goal and
an application sent; the evening drifted"), no praise, no advice.
